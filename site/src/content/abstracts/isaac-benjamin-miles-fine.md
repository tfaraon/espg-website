A significant source of error in weather prediction is caused by uncertainties in the initial state vector. The central idea behind data assimilation (DA) is to include observations (i.e., temperature, wind velocities, rainfall, etc) of the weather taken after the initial forecast has been calculated. This allows the predicted forecast to be constrained by observations, which tends to improve the forecasting accuracy dramatically. 4D-Var is a data assimilation technique that finds the initial conditions that allow model forecasts to remain close to the observations over the DA window. (DA windows from 1 to 12 hours have been used for differing application). is usually 6-12 hrs long. 4DVar is used to initialise the forecasts of the world’s leading weather forecast agencies. Optimal 4D-Var requires an accurate gradient or adjoint of the tangent linear model (TLM) of the computational model used to predict the weather. For a variety of reasons, it has been difficult for operational weather forecasting centres to produce and maintain accurate TLMs of the numerical weather prediction (NWP) models used to forecast the weather. A promising approach to make the production of accurate TLMs more straightforward is that of ensemble-based TLMs. Ensemble-based TLMs use ensembles of perturbed model runs to calculate the TLM However, to date, these ensemble-based TLM approaches have targeted one specific time integration methods: a problem as NWP models use a wide variety of time integration methods.  In this presentation I will present a novel method of the finding the TLM of an NWP model using ensembles. This method allows for the finding of TLMs with more numerical integration methods as well as expanding the usability of these methods by allowing more generalisation. With this increased generality of ensemble TLMs it is possible that 4D-Var can more easily be implemented as the standard method of performing DA at forecasting agencies around the world.
