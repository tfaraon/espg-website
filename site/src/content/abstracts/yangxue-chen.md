Port Phillip Bay is a large, fetch-limited embayment in southeastern Australia, where wave conditions can vary substantially across the bay due to differences in shoreline orientation, local fetch and wind exposure. Wave storm conditions at individual locations may therefore differ considerably, meaning that observations from a single site may not represent storm conditions across the wider bay. Comparing multiple locations provides a more complete understanding of the spatial variability of wave storms within Port Phillip Bay.

This study examines the spatial and temporal variability of wave storms across Port Phillip Bay using approximately three years of 30-minute wave records from six wave buoy stations between April 2023 and May 2026. Wave storms were identified independently at each station using a station-specific 95th percentile significant wave height threshold, a minimum duration of six hours, and a 24-hour independence criterion. Wave storm occurrence, duration, wave height, seasonality and associated wind conditions were then compared among stations.

Preliminary results reveal marked spatial differences in wave storm occurrence, seasonality and associated wind conditions across Port Phillip Bay. The wind directions associated with wave storms vary among stations, while relationships between significant wave height and wind speed are strong at some locations but weak at others. These patterns suggest that the response of local wave conditions to wind forcing is strongly site-dependent and is likely influenced by differences in effective fetch and local coastal configuration.

Together, these results show that wave storm forcing within Port Phillip Bay is spatially heterogeneous and cannot be adequately represented by a single wave record. This regional characterisation provides a framework for understanding spatial variability in wave storm forcing within fetch-limited embayment and highlights the strongly site-dependent nature of wave storm conditions across Port Phillip Bay.
