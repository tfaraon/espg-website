The Archean Youanmi gold deposit hosts gold mineralization in shear zones spatially associated with the contact between a sagducting greenstone succession and voluminous granitic intrusions. Gold-hosting shear zones formed in the greenstone succession, which consists of mafic to felsic volcaniclastic rocks interlayered with banded iron-formations (BIFs) and basalt. Petrographic and field observations suggest that competency contrasts from the micro-to-macro scale focused ore fluids and gold mineralization, and that gold-rich horizons are highly localized within meter-scale shear zones. Early Au-bearing quartz veins within the shear zone were boudinaged and recrystallized, which liberated gold to the matrix where it was scavenged by fluids that preferentially migrated along rheological boundaries. Units that are coarsely layered appear to have accommodated higher amounts of strain and are highly mineralized in comparison to the more massive basalts. Preliminary structural data and textural features of the shear zones will be presented to provide insight into how these shear zones have developed over time and relate to high-grade gold mineralization.
