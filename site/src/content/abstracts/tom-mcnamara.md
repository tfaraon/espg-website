Muon radiography (muography) is a geophysical imaging technique that uses naturally occurring cosmic-ray muons (energetic particles that are constantly generated by interactions in the atmosphere) to map density variations in the upper crust overlying a muon detector. Muons travel along ray paths through solid materials, and lose energy proportional to the density of the material traversed. Counting muon arrivals (muon flux) from different directions over time reveals a map of the overlying mass along each ray path (muon opacity), resulting in an image of the crust similar to an X-ray.

Most existing research on muographic methods has focused on computing three-dimensional models of the density distribution using differences between multiple detector perspectives (muon tomography). This method has been developed through extensive application to supracrustal features, mainly volcanoes and human infrastructure. It is being increasingly adopted for resource exploration settings and engineering assessments for tunnelling.

There has been little development towards understanding the geological signals in the pre-inversion opacity data, or their interpretation. This presentation introduces muography, and briefly shows some data corrections, analytical, and interpretative methods for handling opacity data to interpret geological features both qualitatively and quantitatively, similarly to how potential field or seismic data are treated, to build a case the muographic data from a single detector source can be geologically informative.

A background correction similar to the gravity Bouguer correction can be applied to isolate density anomalies, and can be applied analytically to interrogate features’ density or volume. Opacity datasets are recorded in angular coordinates that map naturally onto a stereonet for analysis post-background correction. Methods for integration with gravity as a constraint are shown, as well as for interpreting and estimating unit thickness or lateral density variations, plane strike and dip, truncation against a surface, and fold axis strike and plunge.
