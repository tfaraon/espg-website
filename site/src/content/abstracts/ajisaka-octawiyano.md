The Werribee River forms a Quaternary fan-shaped fluvial deposit that underpins regional agriculture in the Werribee Irrigation District, notable landforms at the K-Road Cliffs, and Aboriginal archaeological records. This sensitive landscape in southeastern Australia preserves features such as incised valleys, paleochannels, and fluvial terraces which are crucial to reveal the river system's response to the changing climate throughout the Quaternary. To reconstruct these evolutionary phases, this study integrated high-resolution LiDAR remote sensing, targeted sediment sampling, granulometry, geochemical analysis, and optically stimulated luminescence dating. This multi-proxy approach revealed the geomorphic evolution and hydrodynamical regime change of the river system from \~194 ka to the present day.
