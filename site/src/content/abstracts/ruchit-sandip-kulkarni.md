Drought is rarely the product of a single cause: it emerges from the interaction of large-scale atmospheric pressure patterns, lack of moisture transported into the region by winds, and land-surface feedback that reinforces dry conditions once established. In this presentation, I will talk about the atmospheric pressure, moisture, and temperature patterns identified during different stages of meteorological drought such as development, intensification, and recovery, in different parts of the world.

Using ERA5 and GPCC global precipitation datasets, drought phases are defined using the Non-Parametric Drought Index (NPDI), which is based on rainfall alone, using a percentile approach. I specifically look at multi-year droughts using the NPDI-24 timescale and aim to understand the atmospheric mechanisms operating on monthly and seasonal timescales during the development, intensification, and recovery stages of drought.

The observational findings will subsequently inform benchmarking of CMIP6 and CMIP7 model simulations. I will particularly focus on net-zero and overshoot emissions pathways, assessing whether current-generation climate models reproduce the physical mechanisms driving drought. This would be a prerequisite for trusting model projections of future drought risk under stabilising and overshoot-peak-and-decline global temperature pathways.
