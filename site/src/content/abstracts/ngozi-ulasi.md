Unravelling the drivers of Pleistocene ice-age cycles has long been a major preoccupation of palaeoclimatologists. Fundamental to its resolution is the question of timing: establishing a robust age scale that is, ideally, free of orbital assumptions and, preferably, based on independent radiometric evidence. Over the last two decades, speleothems have played a major role in attempts to anchor ice-age cycles in radiometric time, bolstered by advances in geochronology and mass spectrometry. However, these efforts have been largely restricted to the last 600 kyr due to limitations of the principal geochronometer, U-Th. Beyond this limit, U-Pb dating can be used but this requires speleothems with a number of specific qualities: high concentrations of 238U (\> a few ppm) for sufficient in situ production of 206Pb; well-constrained initial 207Pb/206Pb to estimate non-radiogenic sources of Pb (common Pb); and robust estimates of initial 234U/238U disequilibrium. Few speleothems meet these criteria. Speleothems from Corchia Cave (Italy) have provided one of the few records of ice-age cycles beyond the limits of U-Th dating, providing ages of Terminations XII and X.

In this study, we will present a new U-Pb dated speleothem record from a Corchia Cave stalagmite that grew between 1.17 and 1.29 Ma, encompassing Terminations XVII and XVIII. These two terminations are well captured in ocean sediments from site U1385 (Iberian margin), ‘upstream’ of the cave site (in terms of the predominant atmospheric circulation). Speleothems do not directly record a termination but instead preserve temperature and hydrological changes at the cave site that were triggered by ice-sheet collapse. We combined ocean (planktic δ18O and alkenone SSTs) and climate proxy data (stable isotopes, clumped isotopes, trace elements) from both archives to synchronise the ocean-core record of the two terminations to the speleothem chronology. We then discuss the likely insolation forcing of the two terminations in the context of prevailing theories of Early Pleistocene ice-age cycles.
