The Cardinia CCS project, located in Australian Commonwealth Greenhouse Gas Assessment Permit G-15-AP, aims to provide geological storage of CO2 emissions from international and Australian domestic sources. Situated approximately 150 kilometres off the Pilbara Coastline within the Beagle Sub-basin on Australia’s North West Shelf, the project lies within Australian Commonwealth Waters with geological storage targets approximately 2,500 metres below the sea floor.

G-15-AP was awarded to InCapture Pty Ltd and Joint Venture Partners (JVP) in August 2024. Currently in the second year of the Primary Period, the JVP and technical program partner Halliburton are focusing on technical due diligence required for the submission of the Australian Commonwealth Government’s Declaration of Identified Storage Formation (DoSF).

As an initial step in the lead up to DoSF submission, consideration and evaluation of the integrity of the on-block and surrounding legacy wells is critical for the project’s progression. To determine the impact of legacy wells on the project, evaluation of barrier and integrity assessment, injected CO2 flow pathway simulations, and an overall risk assessment has been completed.

There are nine hydrocarbon exploration wells located within G-15-AP, dating back to 1971, with the most recent drilled in 2007. All wells have been assessed, with those within the identified storage structures considered devoid of hydrocarbon-bearing intervals. All of the on-block wells have been plugged and abandoned according to regulations and drilling standards that existed at the time of abandonment.

The storage sites within the permit boundary aim to provide geological storage of CO2 hosted in saline aquifers, and in doing so will offer CO2 emissions solutions to the “hard to abate” industries from Australia and internationally.

Alternatively, depleted hydrocarbon fields bear considerable data and information, however, well remediation can be costly or not even possible. Saline aquifers on the other hand pose a different challenge, with generally a far lower well density, less recorded information, and the well plugged and abandoned in a “fit for purpose” manner at the time of exploration. Due to the lower well count in saline aquifers, legacy wells need to be thoroughly understood to assess whether they can be remediated, or if new injection wells should be located to avoid plume migrations intersecting them altogether.

The initial step in this legacy well study is an in-depth integrity and barrier assessment to highlight aspects of the wellbore that may require future well re-entry and intervention. As part of this, it is important to consider the well’s barriers from the well abandonment schematics: do plugs align with caprocks? do caprocks align with casing or open wellbore? is the wellbore completely isolated? As part of the Cardinia Project in G-15-AP workflows are used to guide the determination of barrier effectiveness and compliance.

As well integrity risk is a multi-faceted and context dependent factor, there is not a single criterion for assessing well integrity risk. As such, in addition to the specific criteria described in Figure 1, other aspects that are considered when appraising potential plume intersection with legacy wells include:

1. Estimated potential efflux rates.
2. Temperatures and pressures of the borehole during injection.
3. High salinity fluids that may accelerate corrosion of cement, casing, liners and other components of a well bore.
4. Poor cementing or poor class/grade cement leading to cement degradation.
5. Steel corrosion caused by carbonic acid.
6. Cap rock integrity versus well integrity.
7. Natural permeability reduction potential.
8. Natural CO2 seepage analogues.
9. Legacy well barrier efficacy monitoring.
10. Field evidence of methane plumes (if any).
11. Environmental consequences.
12. Remediation HSE risks.

In assessing these legacy wells, not only is the well-specific information interrogated, but the surrounding geology of the saline aquifer, overlying and underlying aquitards and adjacent aquifer/aquitard pairs must also be considered, with data from legacy wells critical in guiding this work. For each well, injected CO2 flow simulations over a reasonable period incorporating available reservoir data (e.g. pressure, temperature, porosity and permeability) and wellbore data (e.g. borehole type, fluid flow pathways, geomechanical modelling, cement plug location, and cement bond logs) are also assessed.

These assessments and simulations are collated into a Well Integrity Assessment, which ultimately lead to the final decision on how to progress each well within the framework of the Cardinia Project. The impact of different outcomes is considered from economic, geological, and regulatory perspectives, considering not only containment but also environmental impacts.
