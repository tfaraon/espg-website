Understanding the timing, magnitude and behaviour of coastlines during past periods of rapid sea-level rise is crucial for understanding future change. Narrm (Port Phillip Bay) is a shallow marine embayment adjacent to Melbourne, one of Australia’s most populated cities.  Presently, quantitative geological evidence for the Holocene marine transgression in Port Phillip Bay is limited to few studies. Radiocarbon dating of sediment cores from the embayment show the post-glacial marine transgression began \~8290 years ago (Holdgate et al., 2001). Earth-ice modelling suggests this part of the Australian coastline experienced a relative sea-level highstand of \~1.5-2m above present \~4000-6000 years ago. One sea-level index point collected in the state of Victoria supports this: fossil barnacles in growth position on a coastal notch \~1.5m above present mean sea level (dating \~6000 years) (Gardner et al., 2009). A sediment record from the margins of Port Phillip Bay also indicates marine transgression beginning \~8000 years ago (Kennedy et al., 2020). We compiled published and unpublished relative sea-level proxy data (with sufficient vertical and chronological resolution uncertainties) to generate a new paleo sea-level curve using a standardised approach (Khan et al., 2019). We also present the preliminary findings from new proxy data collected from three coastal wetlands around Port Phillip Bay. Sedimentological analysis, and microfossil analysis (specifically foraminifera) alongside radiocarbon dating will be applied to reconstruct past coastal dynamics and relative sea-level changes. First Nations People were permanently living in this area throughout this period of rapid coastal change, enhancing our understanding of the past landscapes may provide valuable information for cultural heritage. We anticipate this project will provide crucial understanding of the evolution of the Narrm coastline during the Holocene by providing a recent geological analogue for future sea-level rise.
