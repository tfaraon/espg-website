Groundwater is under increasing pressure in Haryana due to intensive irrigation, industrial development and growing water demand. Panipat District, an agriculturally and industrially important part of the Indo-Gangetic Plain, is classified as an over-exploited/critical groundwater area. However, continuous spatial information on groundwater level (GWL) is limited. This study develops a reproducible Google Earth Engine (GEE)-based machine-learning framework to classify and spatially map groundwater levels across Panipat District.

A Random Forest classifier was trained using 778 valid well samples derived from the Central Ground Water Board monitoring network. Ten satellite- and terrain-derived predictors were integrated at 250 m resolution, including elevation, slope, aspect, rainfall, NDVI, land cover, soil texture, geomorphology, drainage density and topographic wetness index (TWI). An 80/20 hold-out validation was used to assess model performance. The model achieved an overall accuracy of 73.33% with a Kappa coefficient of 0.4775. Elevation, rainfall and NDVI were identified as the most influential predictors, while the six leading variables together accounted for 89.3% of total Random Forest variable importance.

The resulting three-class GWL map was dominated by Low- and Medium-GWL zones, with isolated High-GWL areas mainly occurring in southwestern lowlands and along the eastern Yamuna floodplain margin. These patterns are broadly consistent with the hydrogeological setting and known groundwater stress of the district. The study demonstrates the potential of GEE, remote sensing, GIS and machine learning for reproducible district-scale groundwater assessment and provides a framework that can be adapted for groundwater studies in other regions.
