Carbonatites are uncommon, carbonate-rich igneous rocks that can reveal details about mantle geochemistry and alkaline magmatism. In Finland, carbonatite localities include the Siilinjärvi, Sokli, Kortejärvi and Laivajoki carbonatite complexes. This project aims to investigate the petrology, timing and geochemical evolution of these rocks utilising a combination of petrographic and geochemical techniques, including both major-, trace-element and radiogenic isotope analysis. The Finnish samples show diverse textures, ranging from massive crystalline textures to locally, highly deformed zones. The carbonatite mineralogy is dominated by calcite and dolomite, while phlogopite, amphibole, clinopyroxene, apatite, and oxide minerals are important accessory phases. Back-scattered electron imagery and qualitative geochemical analysis have allowed the identification of minor mineral phases such as ilmenite and magnetite. Future work will employ electron probe microanalysis and laser ablation inductively-coupled plasma mass spectrometry to obtain major- and trace-element compositions of key mineral phases that will contribute to the better understanding of the petrogenesis and dating of these Finnish carbonatite localities.
