Pekalongan is a city located in the northern Java coastal landscape, Indonesia. This city is highly vulnerable to climate- and coastal-related disasters, particularly sea level rise, land subsidence, and tidal flooding. Indonesian National Agency for Disaster Countermeasures notes that floods, including coastal flooding, are the most frequent natural disaster in Pekalongan City, with 37 flood events in the last ten years. In 2020, Pekalongan experienced major tidal floods and inundated up to 477.57 hectares of land, mainly located in residential areas. Tidal flooding in Pekalongan is driven by land subsidence and high sea level rise, and these are influenced by groundwater overextraction, accelerate a rapid land subsidence over time. The main reasons for groundwater overextraction are increasing water demand and low availability of surface water with good quality. This study aims to estimate water demand for domestic use, using Pekalongan Coastal City as a case study. Water demand is calculated based on the Indonesian National Standard related to spatial water balance calculation. Spatial distribution is identified using Geographic Information Systems through spatial analysis of total water demand in each subdistrict in Pekalongan City, using vector data of subdistrict administrative boundaries as the mapping unit. As a medium-sized city, the coefficient of daily water demand in Pekalongan is 120-125 litre/person. The results showed that total existing water demand for domestic use in Pekalongan City can reach up to 40,587,500 litre/day. The highest demand is for bathing (45.10%), followed by laundry (18.46%), and toilet flushing (12.01%). In terms of spatial distribution based on Geographic Information Systems mapping, water demand is highly concentrated in West Pekalongan, reaching 30.43% of total daily water demand in Pekalongan City, followed by North Pekalongan (25.13%), South Pekalongan (22.51%), and East Pekalongan (21.93%). The results emphasise that total water demand increases due to increased population numbers, enhancing pressure on clean water security and groundwater dependency in Pekalongan City, as 77.89% of clean water for domestic use is sourced from groundwater. Implementing circular water use and integrated urban water management can be implemented to support daily water demand and reduce groundwater dependency in Pekalongan City.
