X-ray micro computed tomography (micro-CT) is a non-destructive technique used for reconstructing internal structure and has been actively applied to meteorite research since the late 2000s \[1–3\]. In recent years, it has become a primary non-destructive analytical method in planetary materials science, as evidenced by its application to asteroid samples returned from Ryugu \[4–6\] and Bennu \[7,8\] by the Hayabusa2 and OSIRIS-REx missions, respectively.

However, micro-CT data have the disadvantage that artifacts can arise due to factors such as non-ideal configurations for the properties of the analysed sample, or machine- and acquisition-dependent artifacts \[9\]. While the former can be covered by adjusting the measurement conditions or applying software corrections, the latter cannot be corrected. These artifacts must be considered during image processing and quantitative measurements, which can be particularly problematic when analysing small samples, such as mission-returned samples. Therefore, even when the same model of equipment is used, it is challenging to compare the results unless the scan conditions are consistent. As a result, research data obtained from third-party institutions cannot always be directly compared with data obtained from one’s own research.

The aim of this study is to review the frequency of micro-CT utilization and scan settings used for the purpose of characterizing extraterrestrial materials. Based on this, a guideline for measurement conditions that can provide the best image quality according to the characteristics of the samples being analyzed will be established. Through this approach, the usefulness of data obtained from future sample analyses for subsequent research can be enhanced, supporting upcoming sample return mission sample analysis, and the acceleration of progress in planetary science.

The study focuses on analyzing the analogue of studied sample types, equipment models that have been used, and measurement conditions reported in over 50 published studies that have utilized micro-CT for the analysis of meteorite and asteroid samples. In addition, scans are planned to be conducted on several samples under multiple scanning conditions to compare image resolution and to compile the settings that provide the best image quality according to the characteristics of each sample. For sample scanning, the Phoenix Nanotom M micro-CT scanner (Waygate Technologies) is being used in the Trace Analysis for Chemical, Earth and Environmental Sciences (TrACEES) laboratory at the University of Melbourne.
