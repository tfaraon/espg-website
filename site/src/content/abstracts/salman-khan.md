Indoor environmental quality in classrooms directly affects students' health, comfort, and learning outcomes. Ventilation rate (VR) is a key determinant of indoor air quality, as it governs the dilution and removal of air pollutants and is typically expressed as the number of air changes per hour. Various organizations worldwide have recommended minimum ventilation targets to safeguard occupant well-being, and in the post-COVID era, carbon dioxide (CO2) sensor data have been increasingly used as a low-cost, scalable proxy for estimating ventilation in occupied spaces. In this regard, our study presents a data science-based pipeline that uses CO2 and occupancy sensor data to automate ventilation rate estimation. The method assumes well-mixed indoor conditions and requires only a CO2 sensor and corresponding occupancy data per room. Raw CO2 and occupancy signals are first cleaned and then segmented into one of three regimes — equilibrium, buildup, and decay — and VR is estimated from the corresponding mass-balance formulation for that regime. An automated two-stage screening procedure also flags sensors that are faulty or poorly placed. The pipeline requires no manual tuning per room and scales with low computational overhead to hundreds or thousands of monitored spaces across a campus. As a first validation, the method was applied to six classrooms in a university building across the 2024 and 2025 academic years. The calculated VR ranged from 0.28–12.42 h⁻¹, averaging 5.8 h⁻¹ (SD = 1.2), compared against actual VR, taken from the building management, which averaged 6.4 h⁻¹ (SD = 0.96). The sensor-based method showed a small but consistent underestimation. Work is ongoing to extend the analysis to buildings across campus. The approach offers a practical route toward continuous, automated indoor-air-quality monitoring that can inform facilities interventions and support healthier learning environments for students.
