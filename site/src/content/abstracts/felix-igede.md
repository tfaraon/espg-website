The relationship between fault architecture and the subsurface organisation of seafloor massive sulphide (SMS) systems remains poorly constrained. The JIM hydrothermal field, within the German BGR exploration licence area on the Southeast Indian Ridge, occupies an uplifted inner-corner high adjacent to a NW-SE-oriented non-transform discontinuity. Its associated depression extends approximately 20 km at water depths of 4100-4400 m, separating the ridge segments hosting JIM and PELAGIA. This study assesses how fault architecture and ridge segmentation relate to sulphide localisation and exploration potential at JIM.

High-resolution multibeam bathymetry, geological sampling, remotely operated vehicle observations, stereonet analysis and vertical throw profiling were integrated with three-dimensional structural and magnetic modelling. Fifteen active and inactive sulphide sites occur within a network of four major normal faults (F1-F4) and subsidiary structures (M1-M9), defining dominant NW-SE and NE-SW fault sets. Vent sites preferentially align with F1 and F2, particularly where subsidiary faults M1 and M2 intersect them. Throw profiles along F1-F3 show multiple maxima of approximately 200-350 m, separated by interpreted relay ramps and linkage zones. Modelled fault surfaces extend approximately 2.5 km below the seafloor, and interpreted intersections between major and subsidiary faults, including M2-F1, suggest potential hydrothermal pathways. Sites 3 and 4 overlie the largest modelled alteration domain, approximately 1.2 km long, 0.7 km wide and 0.5 km in vertical extent, suggesting a hydrothermal footprint exceeding the exposed mounds without establishing continuous sulphide mineralisation. Magnetic lows coincide with vent clusters, while additional modelled lows beneath a fractured southern depression identify a target for further investigation.

The structural setting of JIM shares features with TAG, Rainbow and Longqi, where faults help localise hydrothermal activity. At JIM, the alignment of sulphide sites with faults and the presence of magnetic anomalies consistent with hydrothermal alteration suggest structurally focused fluid flow and identify targets for further exploration. These results support the use of integrated structural and magnetic analysis for investigating SMS systems along intermediate-spreading ridges.
