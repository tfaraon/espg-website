Kimberlites are the deepest mantle-derived melts that reach Earth’s surface, recording the composition of the upper mantle. However, their primary compositions are often obscured by significant assimilation of mantle material and post-emplacement alteration. While kimberlitic minerals can have variable morphology, rare, spherical, orbicule-like structures are occasionally present, yet they remain significantly understudied. These structures may provide important clues for the reconstruction of melt evolution and ascent processes. This project analyses the mineralogy, textures, and major and trace element geochemical composition of orbicular kimberlite occurrences in Finland, Tanzania, and Namibia. By combining detailed petrographic and mineral geochemical analysis of orbicule-like structures, this study interprets possible formation processes, environments, and timing. Preliminary findings show that the samples feature zoned olivine megacrysts whose degree of alteration varies between occurrences, rimmed by zoned spinel, perovskite, and/or ilmenite, within a carbonate-rich matrix. The specific mineralogy and geochemical composition also vary by location. These observations indicate that each locality experienced a distinct geochemical evolution, and suggests that fractional crystallisation, formation environment instabilities, and magma mixing might have occurred during the formation of kimberlitic orbicules. In addition, it appears that orbicular structures may remain less affected by the alteration that typically affects kimberlites, preserving critical insights for the evolution of these deep-mantle magmas.
