Volcanic ash (tuff) from explosive eruptions offers insights into past volcanic eruptions and serve as important time parallel markers in establishing and correlating the timescales of terrestrial and marine sedimentary records. The Turkana Basin of southwestern Ethiopia and northwestern Kenya provides an exceptional setting for studying these critical chronostratigraphic markers. Along the northern, eastern, and western margins of Lake Turkana, the Omo Group exposes thick volcano-sedimentary sequences that document more than 300 eruptions during the Plio-Pleistocene. The Turkana Basin is also world-renowned as a record of hominin evolution, where fossil discoveries are intercalated with volcanic horizons. This study presents new high-resolution 40Ar/39Ar geochronology, single-crystal geochemical data from the Silbo and Kale Tuffs of the Omo Group, and isotope geochemistry. These units exhibits a distinct geochemical signature, pointing to a source that differs from earlier eruptive sequences. In addition, single-grain ages indicate trans-crustal ‘mush’ residence times on the order of tens of thousands of years prior to eruption. Together, this integrated dataset enhances correlation of closely spaced tuff horizons across the Omo Group and provides new temporal constraints for hominin evolution.
