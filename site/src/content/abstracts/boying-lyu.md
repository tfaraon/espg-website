Temporally compounding drought-to-wet events (TCDW) describe conditions in which prolonged precipitation deficits are followed by an abrupt transition to intense precipitation or flooding within a defined time window. TCDW events are associated with amplified impacts compared with either droughts or floods occurring in isolation. In Australia, despite extensive study of individual droughts, floods, and rainfall variability, TCDW events have received relatively little attention. Existing TCDW identification methods rarely consider the distinct timescales of drought persistence and the subsequent rapid transition to wet conditions. Meanwhile, ENSO variability in rainfall has been extensively studied, but its role in sequences of drought-to-wet events has not yet been explored systematically. In this study, we first developed a framework for identifying TCDW events across multiple timescales and explored the spatial distribution and changes in transition frequency, duration, and intensity during the historical period (1960-2024) using the Australian Gridded Climate Data (AGCD). We then analysed the influence of ENSO and other potential climate factors on multi-timescale TCDW events. Preliminary results show an increasing frequency of sub-seasonal drought-to-wet events in northern and western Australia, while seasonal drought-to-wet events show an increasing frequency in southeastern Australia. In addition, seasonal drought-to-wet events occur more frequently during El Niño conditions, particularly in SON and DJF, compared with La Niña and neutral phases. These findings provide insights into temporally compounding droughts to wet events in Australia, with implications for adaptation to hydroclimatic risks.
