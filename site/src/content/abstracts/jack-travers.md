Future changes in Antarctic surface mass balance (SMB) are uncertain, with significant implications for the sign and magnitude of sea level rise over the coming decades to centuries. Key factors influencing SMB variability are large-scale climate modes such as the El Niño-Southern Oscillation and the Southern Annular Mode; the mean-state temperature; and how these covary. However, covariability in climate modes is poorly understood, which creates challenges for attributing their impacts on SMB. I review the current state of knowledge of Antarctic climate mode covariability and possible changes under future warming, with a focus on implications for Antarctic SMB and sea level rise. A key knowledge gap is that climate mode covariability has not been quantified for Antarctica. I outline how a newly developed empirical orthogonal function (EOF) approach can address this gap and provide a framework to quantify key uncertainties about how climate variability will impact Antarctic SMB in the future. This will constrain our understanding of future Antarctic SMB change by attributing changes to independent climate modes, contributing to more robust projections of sea level rise.
